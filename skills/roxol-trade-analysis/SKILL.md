# Roxol trade analysis

## Establish the exchange

- Read the screenshot or description for the two baskets, exact items, quantities, and Robux on each side. Orient `give` and `receive` from the user's perspective using visible offer labels or their wording. Preserve the status, date and time precision, trade ID, and copy identifiers when visible. Distinguish a copy's UAID or collectible instance UUID from its asset ID and serial.
- Resolve consequential ambiguity before a verdict. A cropped Robux field is unknown. Use zero when the complete record shows no Robux or the user confirms none. If the direction, an item, or the Robux amount could change the verdict, ask one focused question or give conditional findings. Keep screenshot totals separate from newly retrieved metrics.
- Assess the exchange that was shown. For a dated completed trade, prefer evidence available at the trade date and prior comparables. Label current metrics as a separate snapshot and later observations as hindsight or corroboration. For an offer or an undated exchange, state which market date the assessment uses.

## Retrieve and interpret evidence

- Use the connected Roxol MCP tools by their advertised names. Start with one `get_trade_context` call for both baskets. Supply only known gross Robux and enable ownership history when copy identifiers are available. For unknown or net-only Robux, treat any tool default as provisional and keep item-only totals until the basis can be established. Read [Connector fields and follow-up tools](references/connector.md) when interpreting compact fields, handling partial results, or choosing a follow-up.
- Calculate fee-adjusted, quantity-adjusted received minus given totals on the Value and RAP bases separately. Identify assigned Value and label fallbacks, estimates and gaps. Add full outgoing Robux to the given total and net incoming Robux to the received total. Apply the 30% fee to gross incoming Robux and add an explicitly net amount once. Recompute from visible line items when a displayed total disagrees. For example, 438k item Value plus 35k labeled after-fee Robux gives 473k received. Against 470k given, you receive 3k net OP even if the screenshot displays 18k. Explain the 15k gap and leave the gross amount unconfirmed if the record only shows net.
- Read [Comparable transactions](references/comparables.md) and use Roxol to benchmark the main item's OP against recent independent deals in the same direction. Use `get_trade_proofs` if the compact sample is too shallow. Verify ambiguous proof terms against their screenshots, normalize the pricing and fee bases, and group reposts and linked legs before sizing the sample.
- Compare both baskets' liquidity using Roxol's demand ratings, observed sale frequency over a stated window and prices from recent recorded sales. Use targeted market history when the compact sample is too short to support the claim. Identify the pieces that carry each basket, and explain how fillers, adds and net Robux affect the usefulness of the OP and the work of reselling or retrading. Explain the practical trade-off of consolidating or splitting in terms of the user's stated goal, when given. Listing prices are asks and tracked sale prices may be estimates.
- Check both sides for provider-reported projection flags, dropping trends, dated Value changes and sustained weakness in recorded sales. Support material risks with dates and amounts, such as a Value drop or a run of sales below RAP or Value. Attribute flags to their actual provider and label inferences from the history. Explain whether those findings change the verdict, confidence or expected ease of exit.
- Assess tracked copy history separately from the market verdict. Note corroborated changes, timing, coverage gaps and any identity mismatch. Rapid transfers or hidden entries may warrant further review. Ownership history alone cannot establish theft, poisoning, or safety.
- Make targeted follow-ups only when they can resolve a material uncertainty. Stop when the evidence supports the conclusion or further available results are unlikely to change it. If the connector is unavailable, explain what can be established from the record and identify the missing evidence.

## Give the verdict

1. **Verdict and net OP.** Lead with **W**, **Fair** or **L** and **high**, **moderate** or **low** confidence. Immediately give the actual fee-adjusted OP, whether you pay or receive it, the pricing basis and market date, and where it sits relative to comparable deals when supported. Show the fee math when it changes the result and explain any material difference from the screenshot's totals or the RAP result. Give a provisional lean or leave the verdict undetermined when material gaps warrant it. A W or L reflects a meaningful advantage or disadvantage against market evidence. Fair means the terms are broadly in line with the evidence rather than merely close to zero OP.
2. **Proof benchmark.** Show the strongest comps in a Markdown table with **Date**, **Trade terms**, **OP and basis**, **Proof**, and **Why it matters**. State how many independent deals support the observed range and what makes the evaluated trade closer to either end or outside it. If the sample supports only a rough or qualitative comparison, say so.
3. **Liquidity and downside.** Explain which side is easier to move and why, using the specific demand, volume, sale prices and price changes Roxol found. Focus on the items that drive the result and the form of the OP. When the uploaded screenshot already shows both baskets, skip the item-by-item recap. For a written or incomplete offer, summarize only the terms needed to orient the reader or disclose an assumption.
4. **Final assessment.** Weigh the finding that drove the call against the other evidence. For example, explain how a positive resale edge can coexist with a weak premium for the main item in a downgrade. Incorporate the user's goal or copy history when either changes the call. Base confidence on the certainty of the recorded terms, price coverage, and the relevance, independence and consistency of the comps. Explain how unavailable sources or pricing mismatches limit the judgment. Link the specific proofs and market data alongside the claims they support.
